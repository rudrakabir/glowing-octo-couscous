**Part 1 - What IS Sound:** Sound as physical vibration through media, with frequency/amplitude/timbre as properties; **Variable #1 = the source itself** (what vibrates, where, in what space).


**Part 2 - The Complete Signal Chain:**

- **Variable #2 - Transduction/Microphone:** Different mic types (dynamic/condenser/ribbon) and polar patterns (cardioid, omnidirectional, figure-8, shotgun/hypercardioid) control what you capture; placement and "wrong" mic choices create different sonic character.
- **Variable #3 - Preamplification:** Boosting weak signal; clean vs overdriven gain as aesthetic choice
- **Variable #4 - ADC:** Sample rate and bit depth; lo-fi/bit-crushing as creative tools, not just limitations
- **Variable #5 - Storage:** File formats and compression artifacts; datamoshing and generation loss as texture
- **Variable #6 - Digital Processing:** Filters, effects, and extreme processing to sculpt or destroy sound
- **Variable #7 - DAC:** Reconstruction of signal; wrong playback rates and intentional aliasing
- **Variable #8 - Amplification:** Overdrive and feedback loops as controlled chaos
- **Variable #9 - Speaker Output:** Different speakers and resonant objects create wildly different sounds
- **Variable #10 - Acoustic Space:** Room as instrument; re-recording in different spaces

**Part 3 - Alternative Transducers:** Contact mics, electromagnetic pickups, hydrophones, plant sensors, and DIY transducers access different types of vibration beyond air pressure.

**Polar Patterns = directionality:**

- **Cardioid** - heart-shaped, picks up front, rejects back (most common, like SM58)
- **Omnidirectional** - picks up equally all around (ambient recording, room sound)
- **Figure-8/Bidirectional** - front and back, rejects sides (ribbon mics, interviews)
- **Shotgun/Hypercardioid** - very directional, narrow pickup (boom mics for film/TV, isolating distant sources)
- **Supercardioid** - between cardioid and shotgun (tighter than cardioid, some rear pickup)
